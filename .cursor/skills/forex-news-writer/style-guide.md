# PipsAngel news style guide

## Voice

PipsAngel's site is plain, specific and honest. The news should sound the same: a knowledgeable colleague explaining what happened, not a hype account or a textbook.

- Short sentences (under 20 words on average) and short paragraphs (2-4 sentences). Most readers are on a phone.
- Concrete over vague: "CPI rose 0.4% in August, against a 0.3% forecast" beats "inflation came in hot".
- Active voice, everyday words. Explain jargon the first time ("nonfarm payrolls, the monthly count of US jobs outside farming").
- Calm about risk. Markets can move either way; say so without drama.
- US English spelling. Dates in full: 2 October 2026.

## Phrases to avoid

They read as generic AI copy, and the benchmark fails an article that uses three or more:

delve, fast-paced world, ever-evolving, ever-changing landscape, navigating the, navigate the complexities, it's important to note, it's worth noting, in conclusion, game-changer, tapestry, embark on, in the realm of, unlock the, harness the power, dive into, deep dive, buckle up, look no further, rest assured, a testament to, pivotal role, crucial role, the world of forex, whether you're a seasoned, elevate your, unleash, supercharge, skyrocket, landscape of.

Also avoid: rhetorical questions as openers, "In this article we will…", ending with a summary that repeats the article, and exclamation marks.

## Before and after

| Avoid | Write |
|-------|-------|
| In today's fast-paced forex market, it's important to note that inflation data plays a pivotal role. | US inflation data moves the dollar because it changes what traders expect the Fed to do with rates. |
| The dollar could skyrocket if NFP surprises! | A stronger-than-expected payrolls number has often lifted the dollar in the first hour, as in August 2026 ([BLS](...)). |
| This guaranteed strategy helps you profit from the news. | Spreads often widen in the minutes around the release, so many traders wait for prices to settle. |

## Structures by article type

**Event preview** (before a release or decision)
1. Opening: what's due, when (date and time with timezone) and why it matters for currencies
2. What's expected: consensus and previous figure, sourced
3. Why markets care: the link to rates and the currencies most affected
4. How different outcomes have played out before, conditional and sourced
5. What to watch next

**Event recap** (after)
1. Opening: what came out against forecast, and the first market reaction
2. The details behind the headline number
3. What it changes for rate expectations, sourced
4. What it means for traders: volatility, spreads, upcoming events
5. What to watch next

**Explainer tied to the news**
1. Opening: the plain-English answer to the search question, tied to today's event
2. How it works
3. Why it moves currencies, with a recent sourced example
4. Common misconceptions
5. FAQ

## Title patterns that work

- `<Keyword>: what it means for <currency or traders>`
- `<Keyword> preview: <date or month> and what to expect`
- `What is <keyword>? A plain guide for forex traders`
- `<Event> recap: <specific outcome> and the dollar's reaction`

Keep the keyword early and the promise specific. Never promise profits.
